Timestamp evidence

Location proof verification

A timestamp can support that particular data existed by an attested time. It cannot prove that the reported location, event time, or identity is true.

One record, two time anchors

  1. 1. Location record

    Reported identity, time, coordinates, and measurements.

  2. 2. Canonical SHA-256

    Stable field order and rounding produce a repeatable digest.

  3. 3. External timestamps

    A signed TSA response and an OTS hash commitment.

  4. 4. Fresh proof checks

    Recompute the digest and examine the actual proof files.

TSA: an authority's signed time

The .tsr response signs the digest and generation time. Signature, certificate chain, nonce consistency, and policy checks depend on the TSA roots and policies you trust. Certificate validity is checked at that generation time; revocation is not checked.

OTS: a Bitcoin commitment

The .ots proof connects the digest to a Bitcoin Merkle commitment. Online checks require at least two agreeing providers with enough confirmations. Offline checks cannot confirm a Bitcoin anchor; this is not full-blockchain validation.

Choose trust independently. A matching manifest is not proof of authorship. Obtain this HTML and TSA roots from sources you trust; embedded roots reflect the builder's choice, not an independent endorsement.

Archive structure and the exact record binding

The expected input is the mytracker location-proof export format, not a generic ZIP or a server backup. Records and timestamp files travel together so their digest binding can be checked.

The canonical digest includes user, device, tst, lat, lon, and optional acc, alt, vel, cog, batt. Coordinates are rounded to six decimal places; other floating-point measurements to one. Zero-valued optional fields are omitted. Other payload fields are not covered by this canonical digest.

The identity strings and location timestamp are reported data, not authenticated people, devices, or event times. A nonce comparison links the token to nonce metadata in this archive; it does not authenticate the archive's author.

Verification scope, trust policy, and result meanings

Integrity is not authenticity

Entry hashes, canonical digests, counts, and proof references must agree. Someone can change a manifest and its contents together; an integrity pass alone does not authenticate the archive or its sender.

TSA trust

A Time-Stamping Authority (TSA) signs a digest and its reported time. A trust root is a public CA certificate you accept to establish the signer's identity. A policy OID identifies the authority's declared timestamp policy; matching the identifier does not independently audit the authority's conduct.

A strict TSA result requires a valid SHA-256 imprint, signature, critical timestamping-only certificate usage, chain to accepted roots, matching nonce, and an allowed policy OID. Empty policy lists do not mean every policy is trusted. Legacy proofs without nonce metadata may be degraded. No OCSP/CRL revocation check is made, and this browser verifier does not use the browser or OS certificate store.

Bitcoin corroboration

Offline mode checks OTS structure and its digest binding. An unanchored proof can be pending; a Bitcoin attestation remains inconclusive without provider checks. Online mode checks returned header hashes, mainnet proof of work, Merkle commitments, and provider-reported confirmation depth. At least two sources must agree on a mature anchor; six confirmations is the default, not a guarantee of finality.

The selected providers supply chain and tip information. This verifier does not validate parent links, cumulative chainwork, all transactions, or the full Bitcoin chain. Network and CORS failures are errors, not successful checks. Online providers see the browser IP and block queries; the archive is not uploaded.

ResultInterpretation
VerifiedThe proof passed this verifier's checks under the selected trust policy, within the limits above.
DegradedValid evidence with weaker assurance, such as missing nonce/policy linkage or only one mature Bitcoin source.

TSA and OTS have separate results. A valid archive checksum does not imply either proof is verified.
